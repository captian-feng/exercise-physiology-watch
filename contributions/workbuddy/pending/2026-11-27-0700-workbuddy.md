To quantify the effects of repeated-sprint training in hypoxia (RSH) on repeated-sprint performance near sea level and examine the hypoxic dose-response relationship using both frequentist and Bayesian approaches. Eight databases were systematically searched for randomized-controlled trials comparing RSH with matched repeated-sprint training in normoxia (RSN). Best (RSAbest) and mean (RSAmean) repeated-sprint performance were synthesized using frequentist and Bayesian random-effects meta-analyses, with exploratory study-level dose-response modeling. Risk of bias was assessed using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool (RoB 2), and certainty using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework. Twenty-one studies met the eligibility criteria. RSH produced small effects on RSAbest (k = 16; g = 0.24, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.00 to 0.48; I2 = 16.2%) and RSAmean (k = 20; g = 0.31, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.53; I2 = 13.6%). Study-level analyses identified neither a consistent linear or nonlinear association between training-structured external hypoxic exposure and performance nor an optimal hypoxic dose. Future trials should standardize repeated-sprint outcomes and report continuous or high-frequency peripheral oxygen saturation alongside mechanical training output to better characterize the effective hypoxic dose.
